# Vetting publisher sites

Read this before labeling candidates. Marketplace metrics (DA, TF, DR, "traffic") are a first filter, not a verdict: check each finalist with the SEO data provider and a quick look at the site.

## Checks

Run them in this order and stop as soon as a site fails a hard check.

1. **Thematic and semantic proximity.** What does the site rank for, and what do its recent articles cover? The site, or at least the section the article will live in, should be close to the target page's topic and audience. A finance site whose traffic comes from bank statement lookups is "correct, not ideal" for a wealth-management page; say so.
2. **Real organic traffic on real queries.** Traffic and ranking keywords from the provider, in the target country. Look at the trend over 6 to 12 months: a steep drop is a hard fail, a steady rise is a plus.
3. **Ranking distribution.** Number of keywords in the top 3 and top 10, and their evolution. A site with many keywords but almost none in the top 10 carries little weight.
4. **Generalist link farm signals.** Open the homepage and a few recent articles. Reject sites that cover unrelated topics side by side (crypto, washing machines, health, betting), use a stock blog theme with no editorial identity, publish mostly sponsored posts, or rank only for nonsense or exact-time queries.
5. **Outbound links.** Few outbound links per article and few sponsored articles per week. Many outbound links on every post dilute what one link is worth.
6. **For a link insertion in an existing article:** the article itself should get traffic or have backlinks, ideally both. Check its page-level metrics, not only the domain.
7. **Practical constraints.** Dofollow, the number of links allowed, minimum words, delivery delay, acceptance rate, and whether the site already links to the user's site.

## Profiles

**Strict (Tier 1).** All checks apply. Proximity, real traffic, and top 10 rankings are required. Prefer fewer, better links over a longer basket.

**Relaxed (Tier 2, relinking, cheap diversity).** The goal is to pass value to an existing link or to vary the profile at low cost. Proximity still matters, and indexation and dofollow are still required. Traffic and ranking thresholds can be lower, and a modest site is fine if it is clean and on topic. Generalist link farms are still rejected: a spammy page pointing at a good backlink can hurt it.

## Record

For each candidate: label (`buy`, `maybe`, `reject`), price, the two or three facts that decided it, and the provider, database, and date of the metrics.
