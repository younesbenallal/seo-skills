# Editorial guidelines

Use the existing site's articles as the primary style reference. Apply these rules when the site offers no stronger convention.

## Core style

- Get to the point. Prefer concrete, plain language over industry phrasing.
- Remove sentences, adjectives, and adverbs that do not help the reader.
- Use active voice when possible.
- Vary sentence length and keep paragraphs easy to scan.
- Simplify sentences to sound conversational; do not add personality fluff.
- Keep strong opinions only when they help the reader decide.
- Use the brand's language and terms consistently.
- Mention the product naturally when it solves the problem under discussion.

## Human texture

Use these sparingly and only when they fit the established voice:

- natural transitions such as “So, here's the deal…”, “Let me explain…”, or “You might be wondering…”
- an occasional sentence beginning with “And” or “But”
- one-sentence paragraphs for emphasis
- a subtle, professional touch of humor
- a brief fourth-wall line such as “Okay, let's get into it”
- a fair counterpoint: “Sure, X has its downsides…” or “Yes, there are exceptions”
- calibrated hedging such as “probably”, “tends to”, “roughly”, or “in most cases”

For non-experts, gentle approximations are acceptable for non-critical details when they preserve the source meaning.

## Avoid

- dramatic openings, vague hooks, and unprovable grand claims
- repeated definitions, summaries, or conclusions
- fake certainty, fake proof, and invented examples
- dense walls of text
- headings that promise something the section does not deliver
- formulaic transitions between every section
- keyword stuffing or awkward exact-match phrases
- lists, tables, callouts, or FAQs added only for visual variety

## Structure and UX

- Answer the search query early.
- Make each H2 serve a distinct reader need.
- Use 1–5 H2s for a 1,000–3,000 word article.
- Use H3s only to make a complex section easier to navigate.
- Prefer a list for discrete options, a table for true comparisons, steps for a sequence, and a callout for a warning or decision rule.
- Give images and videos a reason to exist in the surrounding copy.
- Use descriptive internal-link anchors and preserve the reader's flow.

## Final pass

Read the assembled draft as one article, not a bundle of sections. Fix abrupt handoffs, inconsistent terminology, duplicated examples, conflicting advice, unsupported claims, and CTAs that appear before the reader is ready.
